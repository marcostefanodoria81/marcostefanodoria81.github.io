Content Operations Diagnostic

Brief or wishlist?

10 questions to find out

Most teams think they are working from briefs. Most of the time, they are not. They are working from requests dressed up as briefs: a topic, a format, a vague sense of what "good" looks like.

A brief is a governance tool. A wishlist is a request. The difference is whether the document tells your team what success looks like before anyone writes a word.

Answer these 10 questions honestly. Count your yes answers.

A
Definition of done
  • 01 When you assign a piece, does the writer know exactly when to consider it finished?
  • 02 Is there a success criterion written in the brief, not just in the manager's head?
  • 03 If two different writers read this brief, would they produce something similar, or something completely different?
  • 04 Do you have a written standard for "this is not publishable" before the piece is written?
B
Scope and context
  • 05 Does the brief name a specific target reader with a specific problem, not just "our ICP" or "our audience"?
  • 06 Does the brief say what NOT to include, not just what to include?
  • 07 Are reference materials (data, examples, sources) attached to the brief, or left for the writer to find?
C
Scale and system
  • 08 Would this brief work if a writer you have never met used it, with no one available to answer questions?
  • 09 If your best writer left tomorrow, could their replacement use the same brief without a drop in quality?
  • 10 Do you have a process for updating the brief when a piece fails, or does every post-mortem stay in the manager's head?
Your score
0 / 10
8–10
You have an operational brief. Your system scales.
5–7
You have a hybrid brief. It holds as long as the team is stable. It will not hold under pressure.
0–4
You have a wishlist. Quality depends on who interprets it, not on the process.

If most of your answers landed in the 0-4 zone, you are not alone. Brief quality is the most underdiagnosed problem in content operations. It tends to stay invisible until something goes wrong.
